# Carter Demo Context

This document defines the speaker roles and AI reaction notation for the live meeting demo. Use these rules when interpreting the transcript and responding to questions about it.

## Speaker roles

- CFO is a human speaker. Treat every CFO line as human meeting dialogue.
- Tom, labeled TOM or TOM (analyst), is a human speaker and the analyst participating in the meeting.
- Mei, labeled MEI or MEI (FP&A), is a human speaker and the FP&A participant in the meeting.
- Carter, labeled CARTER or Carter, is the AI agent. Carter listens to direct queries and the ongoing conversation, then provides AI responses.

## AI reaction notation

- Any text enclosed in square brackets, `[ ... ]`, is a Carter AI reaction, action, or system-stage annotation. It is never a human utterance.
- A blank speaker label followed by bracketed text still belongs to Carter, not to the preceding human speaker.
- When a Carter line begins with a bracketed action, such as `[Interrupts]`, the bracketed text is Carter's reaction and the remaining sentence is Carter's spoken response.
- CFO, Tom, and Mei remain human speakers when they ask questions, express uncertainty, or assign follow-up work.
- Carter may answer a direct query or react to the conversation, but Carter must never be represented as a human participant.

## Scenario 1 - CAC increase

CFO: I'm in the July plan. CAC is up 8% month on month, to $200 a customer. Why?

Tom (analyst): I don't know. I'd have to pull the channel breakdown.

CFO: Carter — why is CAC up 8%?

Carter: Pulling the channel data now.

Carter reaction: [ real query pause — 3 to 4 seconds. Do not edit it out. ]

Carter: ByteDance cost per acquisition rose 20% — $166 to $200. ByteDance is 40% of your paid volume, so that's 6.8 of the 8 points on its own. The rest is mix. Source: marketing spend table, refreshed 6am today.

CFO: So it's ByteDance. Again.

## Scenario 2 - Large claims pattern

CFO: Different question. 3 claims around $300,000 in July. Has that ever happened?

Mei (FP&A): I'm not sure. I'd have to ask claims but I don’t think so.

CFO: Are you sure? I thought this may have happened a few years ago

Mei (FP&A): I really don’t think so but I can check

Carter reaction: [Carter noticing Argument – Activated]

Carter reaction: [Joining claims to policy cohort]

Carter reaction: [ real query pause — 4 seconds ]

Carter: [Interrupts] Not 3 at that size. Closest was October 2023 — two claims over $400,000, both critical illness policies written in 2022, when critical illness was 20% more of your new business mix. July's 3 are the same product line.

CFO: So it's the 2022 book maturing. Not something new.

Carter: That's consistent with what I can see. What I can't tell you is whether underwriting standards changed in 2022. That isn't in any system I'm connected to — you'd need the underwriting committee minutes, and I don't have access.

CFO: Fair. 1 named question, 1 owner. Not another meeting.
